# Adjustments as string diagrams, and where they stop fitting

## With a fixed pattern of label equalities

Fix which labels are equal and which are disjoint, as in a standard
interpretation. Then an adjustment `a : Γ → Δ` sends each variable of
`Γ` to a variable or term over `Δ`. That is a morphism of the free
cartesian category on the term and adjustment atoms, drawn from `Δ` up
to `Γ`:

- **Wires and boxes.** The wires are variables. Term and adjustment
  atoms are boxes.
- **Copy and discard.** `Δ`'s wires may be copied (used several times)
  and discarded (not used). These form the comonoid of a cartesian
  category, natural with respect to every box.
- **Labels.** Each label is an ordered bundle of wires, `h₀` the
  leftmost. Equal labels are the same bundle; disjoint labels are
  different bundles.

The steps are then:

| Step | Diagram |
|---|---|
| `⟦ \ h as _ ⟧` | a new leftmost wire in `h`'s bundle in `Δ`, discarded; the rest of the bundle passes through, one place to the right |
| an entry sending `h` to a term `u` | `Γ`'s leftmost `h` wire is produced by `u`'s box, fed from `Δ`; the rest passes one place to the left |
| `⟦ h as v; g as v \ g as v ⟧` | `Δ`'s `g` wire is copied to feed `Γ`'s `g` and `h` |
| `⟦ g \| a ⟧` | `a`, with `g`'s leftmost wire passing beside it |

The normaliser's rules are then standard laws:

| Normaliser rule | Law |
|---|---|
| cancellation: `⟦ \ h as _ ⟧` then an entry on `h` | the entry's box is attached to a discarded wire, so naturality of discard deletes it |
| eta: a dead entry | the same law at a distance: the wire the box produces reaches no input |
| absorption, the identity renaming | the wire goes straight through |
| entries with equal targets commute | the copy comonoid is commutative and associative |
| steps on ordered labels commute | interchange: they act on different bundles |
| `⟦ g \| ⟦ \ h ⟧ ⟧ = ⟦ \ h ⟧` for ordered `g, h` | functoriality of `⊗`: identity beside a map on another bundle |

**For a fixed pattern there are no "tests" and no order between them.**
A diagram is a graph, and normalising it is ordinary term-graph
normalisation. Dead entries are simply boxes disconnected from the
inputs, visible without any group reduction.

## The difficult example, pattern by pattern

The example with facts (`docs/separation.md`): fact `g < h`, `m`
related to neither. In each pattern the two words give the same diagram,
by a different law:

- **`m` disjoint from `g` and `h`:** `⟦ \ h as _ ⟧` passes `m`'s bundle
  untouched, by interchange. The earlier entry on `m` after
  `⟦ \ h as _ ⟧` then takes the same wire as the later one, which
  becomes disconnected and is deleted by discard.
- **`m` the same bundle as `g`:** the later entry on `m` takes a wire
  the entry on `g` already took. Disconnected again, so deleted.
- **`m` the same bundle as `h`:** then `m`'s bundle is disjoint from
  `g`'s, and the two entries act on different bundles, so interchange
  applies.

No single diagram rewrite covers all three. The same holds for the
14-step example: `z` and `b` the same bundle, or not.

## What does not fit

Our models leave open whether unordered labels share wires, and how. In
the zero-set models a variable may sit in several bundles at unrelated
places. So the wiring itself depends on the model. A string diagram
fixes the wiring, so it cannot express "for every way the bundles
overlap".

Everything difficult (tests, their order, rule 4) lives exactly there.
Each follows a single input through bundles whose relationship is
unknown.

## Literature that does fit: inverse monoids of isometries

Uniformly over overlaps, the structure is an inverse monoid, with
composition written left to right:

- **Isometries.** Each `⟦ \ h ⟧` is an injection `s_h`, and the entry
  without its target is its partial inverse `s_h*`. Cancellation is
  `s_h s_h* = 1`. One label gives the bicyclic monoid; the C*-algebra
  analogue is the Toeplitz algebra of one isometry.
- **Ordered labels** give commuting isometries that also commute with
  each other's adjoints ("doubly commuting", Nica covariance). The lift
  law is stronger: `s_h` is the identity on the *gap* of `s_g`. The gap
  is the idempotent `z_g = 1 − s_g* s_g`, "is `g`'s index 0". The law
  reads `z_g s_h = z_g`. Crisp and Laca study the Toeplitz algebras of
  right-angled Artin groups, generated by isometries that either
  doubly commute or have orthogonal ranges ([arXiv:math/9907026]).
  Our unordered labels are the third case: no relation at all.
- **Without targets,** this is close to the free partially commutative
  inverse monoid. Its elements are Munn trees: a finite prefix-closed
  set of group elements (the positions visited) and an endpoint. Its
  word problem takes `O(n log n)` time (Diekert, Lohrey and Miller,
  *Partially commutative inverse monoids*, Semigroup Forum 77, 2008),
  and later logspace. Our walls and live tests are the backward edges
  of such a tree. The "trie of positions" is a Munn tree.
- **Targets** make it Boolean:
  - A test is the gap idempotent at a position, coloured by its target.
    A decision list `[t₁, t₂, …]` is the join
    `t₁ ∨ t₂(1 − t₁) ∨ …` of orthogonal pieces.
  - Tests with equal colours commute because idempotents do.
  - Boolean inverse monoids and their Stone duality with Boolean étale
    groupoids are due to Lawson, Lenz and Vdovina. Exel's tight
    groupoid is the dual of the tight completion
    ([arXiv:1107.5511], [arXiv:1302.3032],
    [Lawson–Vdovina, JNCG 15]).
  - The points of the tight groupoid are the consistent patterns of
    firing tests. They are exactly what the separation models of
    `docs/zero-sets.md` construct by hand.

**Rule 4 in this language** is an inequality between idempotents,
derived from the lift law. Let `u` be the test on `m` after
`⟦ \ h as _ ⟧`, `t` the test on `g`, and `t'` the test on `m`, as
idempotents. In the facts example:

```
u = s_h z_m s_h*          (inputs whose image under ⟦ \ h ⟧ is m's index 0)
t = z_g,   t' = z_m

t ∧ t' = z_g z_m = z_g s_h z_m s_h*    (z_g s_h = z_g, z_g s_h* = z_g)
       ≤ s_h z_m s_h* = u
```

So in `[u, t, t']` and `[u, t', t]`, `t` and `t'` overlap only inside
`u`, and the two joins are equal. This is a two-line derivation. The
difficulty is only in finding which such inequalities hold.

**A warning from the same literature.** Diekert, Lohrey and Miller
show that quotienting a free partially commutative inverse monoid by
finitely many relations between idempotents gives a decidable word
problem exactly when non-commutation is transitive. The lift law is not
literally such a relation, since `s_h` is not idempotent. But it is the
same kind of relation, and our non-commutation (unordered labels) is not
transitive in general. So deciding rule 4 may be hard in general, not
just hard to implement well.

## What this suggests

- **The target-free skeleton** (positions, walls, live and dead
  entries) is a known structure. It has a near-linear normal form (Munn
  trees, partially commutative), which is the trie the user suggested.
  The eta pass should be "compute the Munn tree", not repeated
  reductions.
- **All of rule 4** lives in the Boolean layer: the order of coloured
  idempotents, and the inequalities the lift law induces between them.
  The question is whether this layer has a decidable, cheap word problem
  for our relations, or whether the lift law has to be weakened or
  replaced. That is the open question.
